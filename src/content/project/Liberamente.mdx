## The research and the reason behind the project

I developed Liberamente as my bachelor’s thesis. I followed a research methodology to
investigate the role of design in promoting health and wellbeing, focusing in particular
on Non Communicable Diseases (NCDs). I found some answers in the salutogenic and
biophilic design movements, but then decided to introduce a new term to define the
specific design field and role I envisioned.

<img src="/immagini/Liberamente_megamap.png"/>

> <h2 style="color: #ed7142;"> WELL-CARE DESIGN </h2>
> Products, services or systems designed to promote a person’s wellbeing in their everyday life which can be implemented at home and in which the person is an active and aware participant.

<div class="text-columns">

## Scenario
Good sleep is paramount for a healthy life and the lack of it has many
implications on NCDs. I delved deep into the topic and identified different
challenges from different population groups. I addressed the following:

## Brief
<h3 style="color: #ed7142; margin: 0%;"> youths + bedtime procrastination </h3>
Plan a ritual for the evening and for the
morning so as to ensure a personal
space to relax before bed and to start
the day in the best possible way.
</div>

## The Solution: Liberamente
A daily practice that involves a relaxing moment
of introspection before and after sleep, guided
by a repetitive hand weaving activity that
stimulates the senses and frees the mind. The
words resulting from the reflection become part
of the structure of the fabric and follow one
another day after day like a potentially infinite
diary. The fabric created acquires a personal
value beyond the quality or usefulness which is not
given importance to.

<img src="/immagini/Liberamente_hook.png"/>
## The Hooked Method
During the development of the project the Hooked method
developed by Nir Eyal to build habit-forming products was
used as a reference for designing the user experience.

<div class="text-columns">
<h3 style="color: #ed7142; margin: 0; margin-top:0;"> Trigger: loom at bed-side </h3>
At first, the loom in shight prompts use; later, the trigger becomes internal.

<h3 style="color: #ed7142; margin: 0%;"> Action: journal and weave </h3>
A journaling activity followed by weaving, motivated by the Endowed Progress Effect.

<h3 style="color: #ed7142; margin: 0%;"> Investment: learning </h3>
Two investment required: learning a new craft and providing personal content.

<h3 style="color: #ed7142; margin: 0%;"> Variable Reward: fabric </h3>
Not only the ever changing cloth, but also the development of a new skill.
</div>

## The Loom

I tweaked the traditional loom in order to both simplify the
weaving, and therefore increase the user’s motivation, and
allow for continued use over an extended period of time.
After the crafting of a few study models, prototypes were
necessary to test the usability of the redesigned loom and
to carry out experimentation to verify the effectiveness of
the designed practice.
Models and prototypes were built with the help of La
Cupertino, a woodwork and carpentry shop where I was
working at the time.

<img src="/immagini/Liberamente_loom.jpg"/>


## Experiment and Testing
<div class="text-columns">
<h3 style="color: #ed7142; margin: 0; margin-top:0;"> Phase 0: participants selection </h3>
The participants were selected based on their answer to the preliminary questionnaire administeres via Google Forms.

<h3 style="color: #ed7142; margin: 0%;"> Phase 1: initial evaluation </h3>
Participants baseline sleep quality was evaluated quantitatively with the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index, and qualitatively with a Sleep Diary.

<h3 style="color: #ed7142; margin: 0%;"> Phase 2: Liberamente in use </h3>
The participants implemented the Liberamente practice for 7 days, during which they continued compiling the Sleep Diary.

<h3 style="color: #ed7142; margin: 0%;"> Phase 3: data collection </h3>
Results after use were collected via the PSQI, once again, and one-on-one interviews to collect more qualitative data.
</div>

## Results
<h3 style="color: #ed7142; margin: 0%;">Improved sense of control and better sleep quality </h3>
During the interviews many participants said to have felt somewhat more in
control of their time which is a core issue in bedtime procrastination.
For most of the participants a noticeable sleep quality improvement was
measured (PSQI) after the implementation of the Liberamente Practice.

#### WHAT I DID - strenghts

As an individual thesis, the project is a true reflection of my skills and my interests.
The topic was my own original proposal, that stemmed from a desire to explore
the contribution of design to society. This project showcases how I can follow up
on my intuition with strong analytical skills and scientific rigor.
It was quite an ambitious endeavor in which I dedicated a lot of time and energy.

#### WHAT I’VE LEARNED - progress

Firstly my main objective was to find confermations and aswers to my intuitions in
regards of the new roles design can have in addressing today’s most pressing
issues. The development of this thesis is what ultimately brought me to enroll in
my master’s. By contrast I’ve also learned the invaluable assets that teamwork
brings, that simply cannot be substituted by individual work.

#### WHAT I WOULD IMPROVE - reflections

As aforementioned I would involve a team, I would also seek experts’ opinion
rather than rely solely on literature. I did validate the design with the final users but
I would try to involve them from the beginning, specifically in framing the problem.
In hindsight I can see how I was already trying to move from a product to a service
that here I called “practice”; now that I’m familiar with service design terminology
and methods I would approach things differently.
