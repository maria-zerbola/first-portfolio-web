## The Challenge
<div class="text-columns">

### Background

In a heteronormative society, it is assumed
that people are divided only into men and
women, with gender identities, roles, and
expressions associated in a rigid and
binary way.

### Guilt-free?

In this context, people are unable to fully
express the entire gender spectrum when
dressing, which generates a sense of
self-guilt.

</div>

## The Solution

A clothes collection that proposes new,
free and fluid associations between
feminine and masculine values, forms,
and expressions, designed for formal
and semi-formal work environments,
which are the ones that most oppose
the new fluid way of conceiving
clothing.

#### WHAT I DID - strenghts

My main contributions to this project were first of all suggesting the issue of
gender expression and guiding the team in understanding the topic, then I took a
key role in conducting the market research by designing the questionairre and
interviewing field experts, finally I led the product development and prototyping
phase because of some previous knowledge in tailoring.

#### WHAT I’VE LEARNED - progress

This project represents my first encounter with the field of innovation. I discovered
a natural predisposition and developed a passion for it. I’ve learned the basis of
innovation dynamics, market positioning, industry and competitive analysis, and
innovation project planning via learning by doing. I’ve had some struggles in
allocating appropriate amount of efforts and resources, erring on too much.

#### WHAT I WOULD IMPROVE - reflections

Though we did conduct expert interviews and identified user’s needs through a
questionnaire based factor analysis, I would instead aplly participatory design
methods to really design a relevant solution. Especially because the
questionnaire was biased as we already had a solid concept. The collection as is,
is unlikely to be adopted. Furthermore I would now answer the brief with a
systemic perspective and not only focusing on the product.
