## Context

The remote <strong>workplace revolution</strong> is bringing benefits but challenges as well: a more <strong>transaction work culture</strong>. It is in the companies’ best interest to improve employees’ satisfaction.

## Brief

How might <strong>remote-first companies</strong> engage their employees in reflecting on and improving <strong>team dynamics</strong>?
</div>

## Systemic Solution

Buzzfield promotes team building in remote-first companies with
the <strong>metaphorical game representation of tending to a vibrant
garden</strong>. Here, <strong>every contribution is a seed</strong> watered and nurtured
through mutual aknowledgement and support.<strong> Challenges like
hovering crows</strong> of miscommunication and stomping bears of
burnout are <strong>detected and addressed early</strong>.
When teams thrive, they harvest jars of sweet honey and <strong>their
impact extends</strong> beyond their workspace, translated into <strong>donations.</strong>

## The Design Process - User Research

<ModalViewer />

### from pain point and opportunities
After initial desk research to understand both the topic of team-building and remote
working, we contacted 12 potential users and conducted one-on-one interviews
remotely. From the informations gathered with the interview we drew a <ClickableImage text="journey map" imageSrc="/immagini/BuzzField_JourneyMap.jpg" />
for each interviewee’s experience working remote. From this we identified pain
points and then focus on needs and opportunities wie addressed in the problem
framing and ideation phase.

### to validation through co-design
After having developed a clear <ClickableImage text="concept" imageSrc="/immagini/BuzzField_OfferingMap.jpg" /> we applied participatory design methods
through a co-design workshop we hold remotely.
This allowed us to focus our effort in developing the most relevant solutions and
discarding the ones that turned out not so useful or too impractical.

## Mindsets - user profiling
The problem statement adress a potentially very wide customer segment, and the non-defined
context of application spans worldwide and across many cultures. 
Therefore, we decided to opt for mindsets instead of personas as a user profiling tool.
<blockquote>
Mindsets prioritize user’s experiences and attitudes rather than demographics. A person is likely to change and move from a mindset to another.
</blockquote>
For each mindset we identified possible evolutions and most importantly what
needs they have and how BuzzField answers to them.

## Service Blueprint
The development of a <strong>user flowchart</strong> and of an extensive <strong>service blueprint</strong>, divided into 4 parts and 6 total
sub-parts, were key to gain clarity and discuss the details of the service designed. It was essential to deal with the
complexity and to communicate both internally and externally.

#### WHAT I DID - strenghts

I guided the inital research by leveraging <strong>interdisciplinary knowledge</strong>, specifically
from the management education I am currently pursuing (MSc). I conducted
some of the <strong>interviews</strong> and led part of the <strong>co-design workshop</strong>; I had a key role in
<strong>synthesizing the insights</strong> from the different sources. Finally I worked on the
<strong>offering map and the service blueprint</strong>. I often was the <strong>spokeperson</strong> of the group.

#### WHAT I’VE LEARNED - progress

With this project I was introduce to <strong>participatory design</strong> theory and methodology,
I’ve learned specifically the importance of <strong>boundary objects</strong> and how to design them effectively.
I’ve had the chance to work on the <strong>storytelling</strong> of both the process and the
concept, specifically in the form of <strong>presentations</strong>, as we often had to formally deliver.

#### WHAT I WOULD IMPROVE - reflections

<strong>Impact measurement</strong> is missing as a very important step of the project.
The breath of the problem statement might cause the solution to not be relevant,
It would be useful to <strong>restrict the problem space</strong>, particularly focusing on a specific
context, ideally on a specific company or role. The visual narrative needs to be improved.
Futhermore I think interviews weren’t as effective as observation/shadowing
could have been. Lastly I would have carried out the co-design workshop at an
earlier stage as well, to <strong>avoid some biases</strong> in favor of our concept.
